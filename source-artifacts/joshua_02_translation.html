The Book of Joshua · Study Translation

Rahab and the Spies

Unit 2 · Joshua 2:1–24

    The Visit and the Lie · 2:1–7

    1 And Joshua son of Nun sent from Shittim two men, spies, secretly, saying, "Go, see the land, and Jericho." And they went and came to the house of a woman, a prostitute, and her name was Rahab, and they lay down there.1234

    secretly — ḥeresh, a rare word; who the secrecy is kept from is open (note 2). lay down — shakav, picked up at v8.

    Moses sends the twelve Num 13:1–3, 13:17–18, 14:6–9; Deut 1:22–28

    At Yahweh's word Moses sends twelve men, one from each tribe, to see the land of Canaan. They return after forty days with fruit and a report. Ten say the cities are walled and the people giants; Joshua and Caleb say the land is very good. The people's hearts melt and they refuse to go up.

    • the verb "send" (shalaḥ), "see" (raʾah), the land, and the report to the leader
    • Joshua himself was one of the twelve
    • "they spied it out" (ragal) in Deut 1:24
    • the melting heart (masas) of Deut 1:28

    Two men, not twelve, sent secretly by Joshua rather than openly at Yahweh's word. They see nothing and report only what Rahab told them, in her words. The hearts that melt are Canaan's, not Israel's (the reversal of Deut 1:28 is this study's reading, offered tentatively).

    The failed mission of Numbers 13 is run again at small scale, and an outsider's words turn it round.

    The two visitors at Sodom Gen 19:1–17, 19:24–26

    Two visitors enter a doomed city at evening and are taken in by Lot. Before they lie down, the men of the city surround the house and demand them. Lot pleads at the door; the visitors pull him in, rescue his family, and send them fleeing to the hills while the city burns.

    • two men, night, a house inside a doomed city
    • "Where are the men who came in to you this night? Bring them out to us" against the king's "Bring out the men who have come to you" (v3)
    • "But before they lay down" against "before they had lain down" (v8)
    • flight "to the mountain … lest" (v16)
    • a family spared

    The host is a woman with no one to protect her but the visitors. She lies where Lot offers his daughters. And these visitors are the ones who will bring the destruction.

    Jericho is named a Sodom, and the story leaves open whether Israel will come as rescuer or as the angel of destruction.

    Num 25:1: "Israel stayed in Shittim; and the people began to play the prostitute with the daughters of Moab." The spies leave the place of the people's last failure and enter a prostitute's house; the link is by place (zanah there, zonah here).
    Matt 21:31–32, tentatively: prostitutes go into the kingdom ahead of the leaders, because they trusted. No shared wording; the shape of a prostitute who trusts.

    2 And it was said to the king of Jericho, "Look, men have come here tonight from the sons of Israel to search out the land."

    3 And the king of Jericho sent to Rahab, saying, "Bring out the men who have come to you, who have come into your house, for to search out all the land they have come."

    The king's demand is the Sodom crowd's (Gen 19:5); see the box at v1.

    4 And the woman took the two men and hid them, and she said, "Yes, the men came to me, but I did not know where they were from.5

    hid — tsafan, "conceal away"; a different verb from the hiding in v6 (taman).
    Heb 11:31: pistei Rhaab he porne ou synapoleto tois apeithesasin dexamene tous kataskopous met' eirenes, "by trust Rahab the prostitute did not perish with the disobedient, having received the spies in peace"; and Jas 2:25, "Rahab the prostitute … having received the messengers" (aggelous), the word Joshua 6:17, 25 uses for these men.

    5 "And it was, when the gate was to be shut at dark, the men went out. I do not know where the men went. Chase after them quickly, for you will overtake them."

    Chase … overtake — radaf and nasag, the pair in Pharaoh's boast.
    Exod 15:9: "The enemy said, 'I will pursue. I will overtake. I will divide the plunder.'" Rahab sends the king's men off in Pharaoh's words; on the roof she turns to the song's other half.

    6 But she had brought them up to the roof and hidden them in the stalks of flax laid out for her on the roof.6

    hidden — taman, hiding out of sight; Achan will use the same verb for the devoted things (7:21).

    7 And the men chased after them on the road to the Jordan, to the fords; and they shut the gate as soon as the chasers had gone out after them.

    The Roof Confession · 2:8–14

    8 And they — before they had lain down, she came up to them on the roof.

    before they had lain down — the exact frame of the siege of Lot's house (Gen 19:4).

    9 And she said to the men, "I know that Yahweh has given y'all the land, and that dread of y'all has fallen on us, and that all the inhabitants of the land have melted away before y'all.7

    I know opens the confession. has given — the perfect: the gift spoken as already done.
    Exod 15:15–16: "All the inhabitants of Canaan have melted away. Terror and dread falls on them … until your people pass over, Yahweh." A Canaanite woman recites Israel's song before Israel has sung it at the Jordan.

    10 "For we have heard how Yahweh dried up the waters of the Reed Sea before y'all when y'all came out of Egypt, and what y'all did to the two kings of the Amorites who were beyond the Jordan, to Sihon and to Og, whom y'all devoted to destruction.

    Sihon and Og: the two Amorite kings east of the Jordan (Num 21:21–35). Reed Sea — yam suf, "sea of reeds".

    11 "And we heard, and our heart melted, and no spirit rose up anymore in anyone because of y'all; for Yahweh y'all's God, he is God in the sky above and on the land beneath.

    sky above … land beneath — Moses' creed, Deut 4:39. no spirit — the same pair returns for the kings at 5:1.

    12 "And now, swear to me, please, by Yahweh, since I have done ḥesed with y'all, that y'all also will do ḥesed with my father's house, and give me a sign of truth,89

    ḥesed — covenant loyalty: the kindness a bond obliges, here asked for and sworn. Left in Hebrew because no English word carries the obligation.
    Matt 15:21–28, tentatively: a Canaanite woman pleads with Israel's teacher for her household and is answered. No shared wording; the same people and the same plea.

    13 "and save alive my father and my mother and my brothers and my sisters and all who belong to them, and rescue our beings from death."

    save alive — the midwives' verb in Exod 1:17. our beings — nefesh, plural.

    14 And the men said to her, "Our life in place of y'all's, to die — if y'all do not tell this business of ours; and it will be, when Yahweh gives us the land, that we will do ḥesed and truth with you."

    when Yahweh gives — still to come, against Rahab's "has given" (v9). our life — nefesh, singular. The plural "y'all" takes in her household.
    Gen 24:49, "if you will deal kindly and truly with my master"; Gen 47:29; Exod 34:6, "abundant in loving kindness and truth." Ḥesed and truth are the language of sworn loyalty, Abraham's servant's and Yahweh's own.
    Judg 1:23–26: Joseph's spies at Bethel promise a man "we will deal kindly with you"; he shows the way in, and he and his family are let go. He sells the way in; Rahab confesses Yahweh, and stays in Israel (6:25).

    The Escape and the Cord · 2:15–21

    15 And she let them down by the rope through the window, for her house was in the side of the wall, and in the wall she was living.

    1 Sam 19:12: "Michal let David down through the window," then deceived the king's messengers: "He is sick." Michal deceives her own father; Rahab, her own king.

    16 And she said to them, "Go to the hill country, lest the chasers meet y'all, and keep hidden there three days until the chasers return, and afterward y'all may go your way."10

    keep hidden — ḥava, a third hiding verb.

    17 And the men said to her, "We will be clear of this oath of yours that you made us swear.11

    18 "Look, we are coming into the land: this cord of scarlet thread you will tie in the window you let us down through, and your father and your mother and your brothers and all your father's house you will gather to you into the house.12

    cord — tiqvah, not the rope (ḥevel) of v15; the same form means "hope".

    Tamar's scarlet thread Gen 38:27–30

    Tamar, who had played the prostitute to secure Judah's line, gives birth to twins. As one puts out a hand, the midwife ties a scarlet thread on it to mark the firstborn. The hand is drawn back and his brother comes out first: Perez, and then Zerah with the scarlet.

    • a Canaanite woman called a prostitute
    • a scarlet thread (shani)
    • tying (qashar): in Genesis through 2 Samuel, the only place tie and scarlet come together
    • a place in Israel's line

    There the thread marks a hand at a birth, and fails to mark the one who comes first. Here it marks a window in a siege, and it marks those who live.

    It sets Rahab beside Tamar, in a line of women from outside Israel who secure the line's survival.

    Matthew's genealogy names both: Perez and Zerah ek tes Thamar, "by Tamar" (Matt 1:3), and Boaz ek tes Rhachab, "by Rahab" (Matt 1:5).

    19 "And it will be: anyone who goes out of the doors of your house into the street, his blood is on his head, and we will be clear; and anyone who is with you in the house, his blood is on our head, if a hand is on him.

    Exod 12:21–23, tentatively: a marked house, the family inside, "none of you shall go out of the door of his house until the morning," and the destroyer passes over; blood (dam) and house (bayit) on both sides, though the wording is otherwise thin.

    20 "And if you tell this business of ours, then we will be clear of your oath that you made us swear."

    21 And she said, "As y'all's words, so it is." And she sent them off, and they went; and she tied the scarlet cord in the window.

    tied … scarlet — the pair of Gen 38:28 (see the box at v18). The third sender in the chapter.

    The Return · 2:22–24

    22 And they went and came to the hill country and stayed there three days, until the chasers had returned; and the chasers searched all along the road and did not find them.

    23 And the two men turned back and came down from the hill country and crossed over and came to Joshua son of Nun, and recounted to him all that had found them.13

    crossed over — the Jordan, unnamed: a small crossing back before the great one.

    24 And they said to Joshua, "Yes, Yahweh has given all the land into our hand; and also all the inhabitants of the land have melted away before us."14

    has given … melted away — Rahab's words from v9, nearly verbatim.

    Notes

    1. one paragraph (2:1–24). The Masoretic text runs the whole chapter as a single paragraph, from a break after 1:18 to a break after 2:24. The four headings here are this study's own scene divisions: the visit and the lie, the roof confession, the escape and the cord, the return.
    2. secretly (v1). The word (ḥeresh) is rare. One reading takes the secrecy as aimed at Israel itself: the first scouts were sent openly and at Yahweh's word, and this time the story never says Yahweh was consulted. Another reads it simply as "quietly", a precaution against Jericho. The text does not decide.
    3. prostitute (v1). Zonah stays her title when the city falls (6:17, 6:25) and in the New Testament. An old reading makes her an innkeeper, and others soften the word in other ways; this study keeps the plain sense.
    4. Rahab and the public square (v1). Tentative. At Sodom the visitors say they will spend the night in the rehov, the town's open square (Gen 19:2), and some hear a sound-play with Rahab's name. The words are different lemmas, so the link is sound only.
    5. I did not know (v4). Tentative. The Sodom crowd wants to "know" the visitors (Gen 19:5); Rahab says she did not know where the men came from (vv4–5), and her confession opens "I know" (v9). A double sense of yadaʿ is arguable but not certain.
    6. the hostess who kills (v6). Judges 4:17–21 turns this scene inside out: Jael takes a fugitive into her tent, covers him, is told to say "no one" if asked, and then kills him. The two stories share a shape (a woman, a hidden man, pursuers expected at the door) but no wording; the reversal of motive is the point of comparison, if there is one.
    7. the fear of y'all (v9). Rahab says the dread is "of y'all", not of Yahweh, though the Song of the Sea it echoes is about Yahweh's arm. One reading takes the shift as irony: Canaan fears Israel, and Israel has done nothing yet. Her confession at v11 puts Yahweh back at the centre.
    8. ban and loyalty (v12). The Torah's law of the ban says "nor show mercy to them" (Deut 7:2), yet Rahab asks for ḥesed and receives it. One reading calls the story a legal precedent for suspending the ban; another notes that the offer of peace in Deut 20:10–15 covers far cities, not these. The tension is real, and the text does not resolve it.
    9. ḥesed in Exodus 34 (v12). One reading notes that at the golden calf, ḥesed is the one quality of Yahweh that stands against the death sentence (Exod 34:6–7), and hears that here: a doomed woman asks for the one thing that could override her city's sentence. Possible, not more.
    10. three days (vv16, 22). The spies' three days in the hills sit beside the three days of preparation Joshua announces (1:11) and the officers' three days (3:2). One reading puts the spies' mission inside Israel's public three days; another calls it a competing count. The text does not decide.
    11. the conditions (vv17–20). The spies set three terms on the oath: the cord in the window, the family inside the house, and silence, with a formula releasing them if any is broken. Readers take this two ways: as the spies looking for a way out of an oath forced on them, or as the legal means by which the ban can be suspended for one house.
    12. cord and hope (v18). Tentative. The word for "cord" (tiqvah) has the same form as the word for "hope", and "scarlet" (shani) sounds like "two" (shenayim), the number of the men. One reader finds a double pun: the hope of the two. The cord reading itself is secure.
    13. messengers and spies (v23; 6:17–25). When Jericho falls the two are called "messengers", "the young men who were spies" and "the two men", and Rahab "lives in the middle of Israel to this day". "To this day" returns at Judg 1:26, where the Bethel informer's city is rebuilt elsewhere. The payoff belongs to the unit that holds chapter 6.
    14. the report (v24). The spies' report repeats Rahab's words about the land melting away, and reports nothing they saw. Sent to "see the land", they bring back a Canaanite's confession.
