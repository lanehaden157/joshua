The Book of Joshua · Study Translation

Rights of Passage

Unit 1 · Joshua 1:1–18

    Yahweh Charges Joshua · 1:1–9

    1 And it came to be, after the death of Moses the slave of Yahweh, that Yahweh said to Joshua son of Nun, Moses' attendant, saying,1

    Deut 34:5 — "Moses the slave of Yahweh died there." Judges opens the same way over Joshua (Judg 1:1), and 2 Samuel over Saul (2 Sam 1:1).

    2 "Moses my slave is dead. So now: rise, cross this Jordan, you and all this people, into the land that I am giving to them, to the sons of Israel.

    giving — noten, a participle: the giving is still in progress.
    Gen 13:17 — "Rise, walk through the land … for to you I will give it." Abraham heard the same command first.

    3 Every place where the sole of y'all's foot treads, to y'all I have given it, just as I spoke to Moses.

    have given — netattiw, spoken as done before anyone has set foot.
    Deut 11:24–25 — nearly word for word: every place the sole of your foot treads, from the wilderness and Lebanon to the River, and no one will take a stand before you.

    4 From the wilderness and this Lebanon, and as far as the great river, the river Euphrates — all the land of the Hittites — and as far as the Great Sea, where the sun goes in: that will be y'all's border.2

    where the sun goes in — the west.
    Gen 15:18 — "from the river of Egypt to the great river, the river Euphrates": the border promised to Abraham.

    5 No man will take his stand before you all the days of your life. As I was with Moses, I will be with you. I will not let you drop, and I will not leave you.

    Exod 3:12 — "I will be with you," to Moses at the bush. Deut 31:6, 8 — "he will not let you drop and will not leave you," Moses to Joshua; Heb 13:5 quotes it to the church.

    6 Be strong and be firm, for you — you will make this people inherit the land that I swore to their fathers to give them.3

    make … inherit — tanḥil, from the root of naḥalah, "inheritance."
    Deut 31:7 — Moses, before all Israel: "Be strong and be firm, for you will go with this people … and you will make them inherit it."

    7 Only, be strong and be very firm, keeping watch to do according to all the torah that Moses my slave commanded you. Do not turn from it right or left, so that you may act with insight wherever you go.4

    Deut 17:18–20 — the king's law: he copies the torah, reads it all his days, and does not turn from it right or left. Joshua is charged like a king.

    8 This scroll of the torah shall not move away from your mouth. You shall murmur it day and night, so that you keep watch to do according to all that is written in it. For then you will make your way succeed, and then you will act with insight.

    murmur — hagah, reading half-aloud, under one's breath.

    9 Have I not commanded you? Be strong and be firm. Do not tremble and do not shatter, for Yahweh your God is with you wherever you go."5

    be strong and be firm — the frame closes: vv. 7 and 9 open identically.

    Joshua Orders the Officers · 1:10–11

    10 And Joshua commanded the officers of the people, saying,

    11 "Cross through the middle of the camp and command the people, saying: 'Get provisions ready for y'all, for in three more days y'all are crossing this Jordan, to go in to possess the land that Yahweh y'all's God is giving y'all to possess.'"6

    y'all are crossing — ʿovrim, a participle: already under way.
    Exod 12:39 — leaving Egypt in haste, Israel "had not prepared provisions." This time there is time to pack.

    Joshua and the Eastern Tribes · 1:12–18

    12 And to the Reubenite, and to the Gadite, and to half the tribe of Manasseh, Joshua said, saying,

    13 "Remember the word that Moses the slave of Yahweh commanded y'all, saying: 'Yahweh y'all's God is giving y'all rest, and he will give y'all this land.'

    giving rest — meniaḥ, the first appearance of the book's word for its own completion.
    Deut 3:18–20 — Moses' original terms to these tribes, which Joshua now quotes back to them.

    14 Y'all's wives, y'all's little ones, and y'all's livestock will stay in the land that Moses gave y'all on the far side of the Jordan. But y'all — y'all will cross over in battle array before y'all's brothers, every mighty man of valor, and y'all will help them,7

    in battle array — ḥamushim, the rare word for Israel marching out of Egypt (Exod 13:18).

    15 until Yahweh gives y'all's brothers rest as he has y'all, and they too possess the land that Yahweh y'all's God is giving them. Then y'all will turn back to the land of y'all's possession and possess it — the land that Moses the slave of Yahweh gave y'all on the far side of the Jordan, toward the sunrise."8

    Yahweh … is giving / Moses … gave — the land credited to both givers within one verse.

    16 And they answered Joshua, saying, "All that you have commanded us we will do, and wherever you send us we will go.

    17 Just as we listened to Moses in all things, so we will listen to you. Only may Yahweh your God be with you, as he was with Moses.9

    Deut 34:9 — "the sons of Israel listened to him and did as Yahweh commanded Moses." The Torah's last word on Joshua becomes the tribes' pledge.

    18 Any man who rebels against your mouth and does not listen to your words, in all that you command him, will be put to death. Only be strong and be firm."

    rebels against your mouth — the verb for the spies' generation, who "rebelled against the mouth of Yahweh" (Deut 1:26). The chapter ends on Yahweh's charge, now spoken by the tribes.

    Notes

    1. And it came to be (v1). wayehi is the ordinary link that carries Hebrew narrative from one event to the next, so the book opens as a continuation rather than a fresh start: Deuteronomy 34 has just buried Moses, and the story walks straight on. The chapter keeps him in view. His name occurs eleven times (1:1 twice, 2, 3, 5, 7, 13, 14, 15, 17 twice); Joshua's occurs four (1:1, 10, 12, 16).
    2. all the land of the Hittites (v4). The phrase is absent from the Greek, one of several places where chapter 1 is longer in Hebrew (also "all the torah," v7, and "to possess it," v11). It matters for dating. The Neo-Assyrian name for Syria was "Hatti," which would fit a later setting; another reading points instead to the second-millennium Hittite empire.
    3. firm (v6). ʾamats is rendered "firm" rather than the customary "courageous." The verb signals determination, as in Ruth's resolve to go with Naomi (Ruth 1:18). The pairing ḥazaq weʾamats reads in some analyses as military exhortation language. The rendering is a deliberate departure from the usual one.
    4. act with insight (v7). Two senses of sakal are in play. More than one early reading takes tashkil as "succeed," a synonym of tatsliaḥ in v8. Another argues for "have insight," keeping the wisdom background, and the Greek supports it ("understand"). Rendered here as "act with insight."
      from it (v7). mimmennu is masculine although torah is feminine, and the Masoretic note records that the feminine would be expected. It may refer to the whole command rather than to the word torah.
    5. A frame around vv. 7–9. One reading takes 1:7–9 as a chiasm: A (v7a–b), B (v7c), C (v8a), C′ (v8b), B′ (v8c–d), A′ (v9). Checked against the Hebrew, the outer frame is exact, since ḥazaq weʾamats opens both v7 and v9. B and B′ share a real word, tashkil ("act with insight," 7c and 8d). The centre pairs two ideas with no shared vocabulary, and the phrase "keep watch to do according to all" links A to B′, crossing the symmetry rather than mirroring it. What the text actually marks is an envelope with a partly verbal middle.
    6. provisions (v11). What tsedah means depends on the manna. One view says it cannot be food, since manna already stored would last until the crossing (5:12), and takes it as travel gear and weapons. Another takes it as food other than bread, since the manna was still falling. Opposite conclusions are drawn from the same verse, and neither is chosen here.
    7. the far side of the Jordan (v14). beʿever hayyarden means "the other side of the Jordan" relative to wherever the speaker stands. At 1:14–15 it names the east bank, even though Israel is still standing on the east bank. The Greek lacks the phrase, along with Moses as giver, in 1:14.
    8. The two givers (vv13–15). Moses' original terms (Deut 3:18–20) say Yahweh gave the east bank. Joshua, quoting them, names Moses as giver twice. One reading takes the alternation as marking the eastern tribes' united-yet-separate status. Another takes it as a quiet demotion of the Transjordan, a gift of Moses rather than of Yahweh. The two readings do not coincide. "Remember" in v13 is zakor, the bare verb used as an emphatic command.
    9. Only (v17). raq restricts. The open question is whether the tribes attach a condition to their loyalty ("we will obey you, provided Yahweh is with you"). One reading takes it as a wish: walk in Moses' way, so that Yahweh will be with you. A related tradition uses the same "only" in v18 to limit "will be put to death," so that it cannot cover an order to sin.
