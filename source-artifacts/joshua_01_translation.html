The Book of Joshua · Study Translation

Rights of Passage

Unit 1 · Joshua 1:1–18

    Yahweh Charges Joshua · 1:1–9

    1 And it came to be, after the death of Moses the servant of Yahweh, that Yahweh said to Joshua son of Nun, Moses' attendant, saying,

    it came to be — wayehi, the narrative waw-consecutive: the book opens mid-sequence, as if Deuteronomy 34 were still being told.2

    2 "Moses my servant is dead. So now: rise, cross this Jordan, you and all this people, into the land that I am giving to them, to the sons of Israel.

    rise, cross — qum ʿavor. Radak reads qum as urgency, not literal standing (compare Gen 13:17; Deut 2:13; 1 Sam 16:12). giving — noten, a participle: the giving is in progress.

    3 Every place where the sole of y'all's foot treads, to y'all I have given it, just as I spoke to Moses.

    have given — netattiw, perfect: spoken as done before anyone has set foot. The address turns plural here ('y'all') and back to singular in v. 5.

    4 From the wilderness and this Lebanon, and as far as the great river, the river Euphrates — all the land of the Hittites — and as far as the Great Sea, where the sun goes in: that will be y'all's border.

    where the sun goes in — mevoʾ hashshemesh, the west. all the land of the Hittites — absent from the Greek.8

    5 No man will take his stand before you all the days of your life. As I was with Moses, I will be with you. I will not let you drop, and I will not leave you.

    I will be with you — ʾehyeh ʿimmak, the burning-bush promise to Moses (Exod 3:12), now given to his successor.

    6 Be strong and be firm, for you — you will make this people inherit the land that I swore to their fathers to give them.

    make … inherit — tanḥil, the Hiphil (causative) of the root behind naḥalah, 'inheritance.' firm — see note 3.3

    7 Only, be strong and be very firm, keeping watch to do according to all the torah that Moses my servant commanded you. Do not turn from it right or left, so that you may act with insight wherever you go.

    from it — mimmennu, a masculine form although torah is feminine; the Masoretic note records that one would expect the feminine.7

    8 This scroll of the torah shall not move away from your mouth. You shall murmur it day and night, so that you keep watch to do according to all that is written in it. For then you will make your way succeed, and then you will act with insight.

    murmur — hagita, reading under one's breath; the same verb and 'day and night' reappear in Psalm 1:2. Rashi and Radak both lean toward meditation of the heart (Ps 19:15).

    9 Have I not commanded you? Be strong and be firm. Do not tremble and do not shatter, for Yahweh your God is with you wherever you go."

    be strong and be firm — the frame closes: vv. 7 and 9 open identically.1

    Joshua Orders the Officers · 1:10–11

    10 And Joshua commanded the officers of the people, saying,

    11 "Cross through the middle of the camp and command the people, saying: 'Get provisions ready for y'all, for in three more days y'all are crossing this Jordan, to go in to possess the land that Yahweh y'all's God is giving y'all to possess.'"

    provisions — tsedah, the noun for what Israel had not prepared on leaving Egypt (Exod 12:39).4 y'all are crossing — ʿovrim, a participle: already under way.

    Joshua and the Eastern Tribes · 1:12–18

    12 And to the Reubenite, and to the Gadite, and to half the tribe of Manasseh, Joshua said, saying,

    13 "Remember the word that Moses the servant of Yahweh commanded y'all, saying: 'Yahweh y'all's God is giving y'all rest, and he will give y'all this land.'

    remember — zakor, an infinitive absolute (the bare verbal idea, with no person or tense) used as an emphatic command. giving rest — meniaḥ, the first appearance of the book's word for its own completion.

    14 Y'all's wives, y'all's little ones, and y'all's livestock will stay in the land that Moses gave y'all on the far side of the Jordan. But y'all — y'all will cross over in battle array before y'all's brothers, every mighty man of valor, and y'all will help them,

    in battle array — ḥamushim, the rare word used of Israel leaving Egypt (Exod 13:18). Radak: equivalent to ḥalutsim, 'vanguard.' far side — beʿever, from the same root as 'cross.'5

    15 until Yahweh gives y'all's brothers rest as he has y'all, and they too possess the land that Yahweh y'all's God is giving them. Then y'all will turn back to the land of y'all's possession and possess it — the land that Moses the servant of Yahweh gave y'all on the far side of the Jordan, toward the sunrise."

    Yahweh … is giving / Moses … gave — the land credited to both givers within one verse.6

    16 And they answered Joshua, saying, "All that you have commanded us we will do, and wherever you send us we will go.

    17 Just as we listened to Moses in all things, so we will listen to you. Only may Yahweh your God be with you, as he was with Moses.

    only — raq, the particle of restriction.6

    18 Any man who rebels against your mouth and does not listen to your words, in all that you command him, will be put to death. Only be strong and be firm."

    rebels against your mouth — yamreh ʾet-pika, Hiphil of marah, the verb for the spies' generation that 'rebelled against the mouth of Yahweh' (Deut 1:26). The chapter ends on Yahweh's charge, now spoken by the tribes.

    Notes

    Hawk reads 1:7–9 as a chiasm: A (v. 7a–b), B (v. 7c), C (v. 8a), C′ (v. 8b), B′ (v. 8c–d), A′ (v. 9). Checked against the Hebrew, the outer frame is exact, since ḥazaq weʾamats opens both v. 7 and v. 9. B and B′ share a real word, tashkil ('act with insight,' 7c and 8d). The centre pairs two ideas that share no inverted vocabulary. The phrase lishmor laʿasot kekol ('keep watch to do according to all') links A to B′, which crosses the symmetry rather than mirroring it. Verdict: a verified envelope with a partly verbal middle. Present it as Hawk's reading, not as a structure marked in the text.

    Hawk counts Moses' name ten times in the chapter and Joshua's four. Lemma 4872 in Joshua-words.tsv gives eleven: 1:1 (twice), 2, 3, 5, 7, 13, 14, 15, 17 (twice). Joshua (3091) gives four: 1:1, 10, 12, 16. Worth rechecking before the note ships.

    'Firm' for ʾamats departs from the customary 'courageous.' Dozeman: the verb signals determination, as in Ruth's resolve to go with Naomi (Ruth 1:18). Hawk and Dozeman both classify ḥazaq weʾamats as military exhortation language (Dozeman cites Rowlett). Deviation flagged for translation-choices.md; Lane decides whether it becomes a row.

    What tsedah means depends on the manna. Rashi says it cannot be food, because the people had manna stored that would last until 16 Nisan (5:12). He takes it as travel gear and weapons. Radak takes it as foods other than bread, because the manna was still falling. He also reports the rabbinic view that the manna stopped when Moses died and what had been gathered lasted until 16 Nisan. Two readers draw opposite conclusions from the same verse; neither is chosen here.

    Dozeman: beʿever hayyarden means 'the other side of the Jordan' relative to wherever the speaker stands. At 1:14–15 it names the east bank, although Israel is still standing on the east bank. The Greek lacks the phrase, along with Moses as giver, in 1:14.

    The two givers. Hawk, following Polzin, notes that Joshua replaces the ambiguous 'I have given' of Deut 3:18–20 with 'Moses,' and reads the alternation as the eastern tribes' united-yet-separate status. Dozeman reports Mazor's reading of the Masoretic wording as disparaging the Transjordan, a gift of Moses rather than of Yahweh. The two readings do not coincide. On raq in 1:17–18, Hawk asks whether the tribes are attaching a condition to their loyalty. Radak reads 1:17 as 'walk in Moses' way, so that Yahweh will be with you.' The rabbinic reading Radak reports uses raq to limit 'will be put to death,' so that it does not cover an order to sin.

    Two senses of sakal. Rashi's note (the explanation is Metsudat Tzion's footnote in the Metsudah edition) and both Targums take tashkil as 'succeed,' like tatsliaḥ. Dozeman argues for 'have insight,' keeping the wisdom background, and the Greek supports him ('understand'). Rendered here as 'act with insight.'

    Rabbit hole, held. The Masoretic pluses in ch. 1 ('all the land of the Hittites,' 'all the torah,' 'to possess it'). Dozeman cites the Neo-Assyrian use of māt Ḫatti for Syria, which bears on dating. Hawk instead points to the second-millennium Hittite empire.
