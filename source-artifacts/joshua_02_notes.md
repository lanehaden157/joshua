# Unit 02, Joshua 2:1-24 ("Rahab and the Spies"), notes file

Research chat, pass 2. Unit map: Movement I (Entry), unit 2 = 2:1-24; it matches the chapter grid. The Masoretic petuhah falls after 1:18 and the one setumah after 2:24, so the chapter is a single unbroken Masoretic paragraph. Scenes below are interpretive. No Masoretic break supports them.

How to read the table. English quotations are from the World English Bible text (the project's own English is the same family) and are VERIFIED, read this chat from the target text. Hebrew lemma ids in the evidence column are Strong-style ids from the project word table (Joshua) and from the Westminster Leningrad lemmatisation (targets); a comparison of the id sets was run by script. Transliteration follows the project scheme. Greek is transliterated with an English gloss, and the glosses are mine. Strength: strong / possible / weak. Rows marked **tentative** are plausible links where the text bears the link out but the support is thin or scholars disagree. The reason is in the verdict.

## Stories

**Scouts (row 2, 54, 53, 28).** Story: Moses sends twelve men openly, at Yahweh's word; Joshua and Caleb return a good report and the people melt. Same: the verb "send" (shalaḥ), "see the land" (ra'ah), the report, hearts that melt, Joshua himself. Different: two men, secretly, from Joshua; they see nothing and report only what Rahab told them; hearts melt among the Canaanites, not in Israel. Does here: the failed mission of Numbers 13 is repeated at small scale and reversed by an outsider's words.

**Sodom (rows 6-11).** Story: two visitors at dusk, a host who shelters them, a crowd at the door, the host's deflecting words, a rescue by the visitors, a flight to the mountains, a doomed city. Same: the two men, the night, "bring them out", "before they lay down", the mountain, the family spared. Different: Rahab has no one to protect her but the visitors, she lies (Lot offers his daughters), her city is destroyed by the visitors' people, and the whole family is spared. Does here: Jericho is named as a Sodom, and the story that follows will decide whether Israel is the rescuer or the angel of destruction.

**Tamar (row 45).** Story: Tamar disguises as a prostitute, bears twins, and the midwife ties a scarlet thread on the hand that comes out first, but the other twin is born first. Same: a woman called a prostitute, a scarlet thread, tying, an Israelite line. Different: the thread marks a hand in a birth, here a window in a siege; it fails to mark the first-born, here it marks those who live. Does here: sets Rahab in a line of women outside Israel who secure the line's survival (and Matthew's genealogy names both).

**Judges 1:22-26 (row 39).** Story: the house of Joseph sends spies to Bethel; a man comes out and shows them the entrance for ḥesed; the city falls but the man and his family are sent away; he builds Luz in Hittite country. Same: spies, a city, one insider, ḥesed, a family spared, "to this day". Different: the man sells the way in; Rahab is recorded as living in Israel (6:25); his city is rebuilt elsewhere. Does here: the book of Judges retells Rahab's deal with a man, and shows what happens to an exempt Canaanite after the conquest.

**Michal (row 14).** Story: Michal lets David down through the window and deceives her father's messengers. Same: the window, the letting-down, the lie to the king's agents. Different: she deceives her own father; Rahab, her own king.
