The study

A literary-unit-by-unit walk through Joshua with Lane, in English. Lane has a little Hebrew, so the English carries the language: transliterate every Hebrew word, gloss it plainly, and never print native script — not in chat, not in an artifact, not in an attribute.

Never assume a term is known from earlier. Reintroduce transliteration and gloss each time. Explain grammar in plain terms when it changes how a verse reads (a participle against a perfect, a causative stem), and otherwise let it go. Grammar that is merely unusual (a masculine suffix, a singular object, a Ketiv/Qere) belongs in a footnote, if anywhere.

Lens: intertextuality first. Where the Torah stands behind a line, where a line or word comes back later in the canon (the Prophets, the Writings, the New Testament), and what the echo does. Then narrative structure, keyword tracing, type-scenes, and the creation–covenant–exile–presence metanarrative. Lane's own examples of what he wants: Rahab next to Tamar (the scarlet thread, Gen 38) and Rahab in Matthew's genealogy (Matt 1:5); "sole of the foot" at 1:3 and the dove that found no resting place for the sole of its foot (Gen 8:9). Prefer Joshua's own markers — Deuteronomistic framing, allotment formulae, Masoretic paragraph breaks, the conquest-summary tension with Judges 1 — over imposed symmetry.

Keep the medieval Jewish commentators (Radak, Rashi) and fine-grained grammar in proportion (Lane, 2026-09-21). They are good material but were running too loud in unit 2. Use them as one voice among several, not the backbone of a verse.

How we work

Read `core-workflow.md` (synced folder) at the start of every unit and follow it: the four passes, the ledger, the standing moves, scope and working style. `joshua_study_style_reference.md` holds every artifact rule. `synced-index.md` lists every synced file. None of them show up in a plain file listing, so search project knowledge by filename before deciding a file is missing. Only the commentaries are plain attachments. Joshua on top of the core workflow

- Earlier Scripture here means the Torah first. In the intertext pass, every tracked thread and notable word gets "where does this first appear in the Torah, and where does it come back?"
- Intertext pass, coverage worth reaching for: every person, place and object at the centre of the unit gets a web search for later reuse, including the New Testament (Rahab → Matthew 1:5, Hebrews 11:31, James 2:25), and for the type-scenes it belongs to (strangers sheltered in a threatened city → Lot in Sodom, Gen 19). The commentaries in resources.md get checked for the intertexts they argue for or against. Far fewer than fifteen links considered usually means the pass was thin; search the web as many times as it takes.
- The ledger's evidence column gives the shared words transliterated, and its source column names where the link came from (the leads sheet, a commentary by name, a web search). Rejected rows keep their reason: they show Lane the work was done, and they keep the pass from keeping only what came to mind first.
- Artifact: get the meta block right, since that's what the pipeline hard-gates on. Expect footnotes to outnumber a handful (style reference §4, Balance). A `retro` entry's `w` is optional here; the port fills it.
- A word with a Torah history or a distinctive later reuse (dread, scarlet, sole of the foot) that survives the ledger becomes a local roots[] entry with an `echo` line (the reference first, then what it adds; style reference §1) or, for a verse-level connection, an `aside.echo`.
- Units 1–2 shipped before the intertext pass existed. Their current echoes and cross-reference asides were drafted in Claude Code, not researched here, and are provisional. When Lane asks, run pass 3 against the shipped unit (its canon-leads sheet is in the synced folder), present the ledger, then deliver a revised artifact. Claude Code re-ports it.
