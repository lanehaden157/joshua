The Book of Joshua · Study Translation

Crossing the Jordan

Unit 3 · Joshua 3:1–4:24

    Three Days at the Edge · 3:1–4

    1 And Joshua rose early in the morning, and they set out from Shittim and came to the Jordan, he and all the sons of Israel, and they lodged there before they crossed.1

    lodged — lun, to spend the night. The camp lodges on the east bank here and on the west bank at 4:3.

    2 And it was, at the end of three days, the officers crossed through the middle of the camp2

    3 and commanded the people, saying, “When y'all see the chest of the covenant of Yahweh y'all's God, and the priests, the Levites, carrying it, then y'all shall set out from y'all's place and walk after it.

    4 Only let there be a distance between y'all and it, about two thousand cubits by measure. Do not come near it, so that y'all may know the way y'all are to walk, for y'all have not crossed on this way yesterday or the day before.”3

    yesterday or the day before — an idiom for “ever before,” kept literal because it returns at 4:18 when the river resumes its course.

    Holy for Tomorrow · 3:5–8

    5 And Joshua said to the people, “Set yourselves apart, for tomorrow Yahweh will do wonders among y'all.”4

    Set yourselves apart — qadash in a reflexive form: the people set themselves apart for what is coming.

    6 And Joshua said to the priests, saying, “Lift the chest of the covenant and cross before the people.” And they lifted the chest of the covenant and walked before the people.

    7 And Yahweh said to Joshua, “This day I will begin to make you great in the eyes of all Israel, so that they may know that as I was with Moses, I will be with you.

    make you great — gadal. Promised here in Yahweh's only speech of chapter 3; done at 4:14.

    8 And you, you shall command the priests who carry the chest of the covenant, saying, ‘When y'all come to the edge of the waters of the Jordan, in the Jordan y'all shall stand.’”

    The River Stands · 3:9–17

    9 And Joshua said to the sons of Israel, “Come near, here, and hear the words of Yahweh y'all's God.”

    10 And Joshua said, “By this y'all shall know that a living God is among y'all, and that he will dispossess, yes dispossess, from before y'all the Canaanite and the Hittite and the Hivite and the Perizzite and the Girgashite and the Amorite and the Jebusite.

    a living God — ḥay, with no article: a God who is alive. dispossess, yes dispossess — yarash doubled for emphasis, and for the first time in the causative: to make others lose possession.

    11 Look! The chest of the covenant of the Lord of all the land is crossing before y'all into the Jordan.5

    is crossing — a participle: the chest is already on the move as Joshua speaks. land — ʾerets, also “earth.”
    Num 10:33 — in the wilderness the chest went ahead of the camp three days' journey “to seek out a resting place” for them. Here the chest goes first again, and two verses later its bearers' feet come to rest.

    12 And now, take for yourselves twelve men from the tribes of Israel, one man, one man for each tribe.

    13 And it will be, when the soles of the feet of the priests carrying the chest of Yahweh, Lord of all the land, come to rest in the waters of the Jordan, the waters of the Jordan will be cut off, the waters coming down from above, and they will stand, one heap.”

    come to rest — nuaḥ, the root of the rest promised in 1:13. heap — ned, the Song of the Sea's word for the piled-up water.

    14 And it was, when the people set out from their tents to cross the Jordan, and the priests carrying the chest of the covenant were before the people,

    15 and when those carrying the chest came to the Jordan, and the feet of the priests carrying the chest dipped into the edge of the water (and the Jordan is full over all its banks all the days of harvest),6

    16 then the waters coming down from above stood. They rose up, one heap, very far off, at Adam, the city beside Zarethan, and those going down toward the Sea of the Arabah, the Salt Sea, were wholly cut off. And the people crossed opposite Jericho.7

    17 And the priests carrying the chest of the covenant of Yahweh stood fixed on dry ground in the middle of the Jordan, and all Israel was crossing on dry ground, until the whole nation had finished crossing the Jordan.8

    was crossing — a participle: a stream of people moving past priests who do not move. dry ground — ḥarav, a dried-out bed.
    2 Kgs 2:8, 14 — Elijah strikes the Jordan with his cloak and the two prophets cross on dry ground, the same word; Elisha strikes it again and crosses back alone. The river parts for a successor, as it did for Joshua.

    Twelve Stones · 4:1–13

    1 And it was, when the whole nation had finished crossing the Jordan, that Yahweh said to Joshua, saying,9

    2 “Take for yourselves from the people twelve men, one man, one man from each tribe,

    3 and command them, saying, ‘Lift for yourselves from here, from the middle of the Jordan, from the spot where the priests' feet stood fixed, twelve stones, and bring them across with y'all, and lay them down in the lodging place where y'all lodge tonight.’”

    lay them down — nuaḥ (causative), from the same root as “come to rest” in 3:13: the stones are set to rest where the camp will lodge.

    4 And Joshua called the twelve men whom he had fixed on from the sons of Israel, one man, one man from each tribe.

    5 And Joshua said to them, “Cross before the chest of Yahweh y'all's God into the middle of the Jordan, and lift up for yourselves, each man, one stone on his shoulder, by the number of the tribes of the sons of Israel,

    1 Kgs 18:31 — on Carmel Elijah builds an altar of twelve stones “by the number of the tribes of the sons of Jacob.” The same counting makes the stones stand for the whole people.

    6 so that this may be a sign among y'all. When y'all's children ask tomorrow, saying, ‘What are these stones to y'all?’

    tomorrow — the word of 3:5: the day of wonders and the day of the children's question share one word.

    7 then y'all shall say to them that the waters of the Jordan were cut off before the chest of the covenant of Yahweh. When it crossed into the Jordan, the waters of the Jordan were cut off. And these stones shall be a remembrance for the sons of Israel forever.”

    8 And the sons of Israel did so, as Joshua commanded. They lifted twelve stones from the middle of the Jordan, as Yahweh had spoken to Joshua, by the number of the tribes of the sons of Israel, and they brought them across with them to the lodging place and laid them down there.

    9 And twelve stones Joshua set up in the middle of the Jordan, under the spot where the feet of the priests carrying the chest of the covenant stood, and they have been there to this day.10

    10 And the priests carrying the chest were standing in the middle of the Jordan until everything was finished that Yahweh commanded Joshua to speak to the people, according to all that Moses commanded Joshua. And the people hurried and crossed.

    11 And it was, when all the people had finished crossing, then the chest of Yahweh crossed, and the priests, before the people.11

    12 And the sons of Reuben and the sons of Gad and the half-tribe of Manasseh crossed arrayed for battle before the sons of Israel, as Moses had spoken to them.12

    13 About forty thousand equipped for war crossed before Yahweh for battle, to the plains of Jericho.

    Joshua Made Great · 4:14

    14 On that day Yahweh made Joshua great in the eyes of all Israel, and they feared him as they had feared Moses, all the days of his life.13

    Up from the Riverbed · 4:15–24

    15 And Yahweh said to Joshua, saying,

    16 “Command the priests carrying the chest of the testimony to come up from the Jordan.”14

    17 And Joshua commanded the priests, saying, “Come up from the Jordan.”

    18 And it was, when the priests carrying the chest of the covenant of Yahweh came up from the middle of the Jordan, and the soles of the priests' feet were pulled up onto the dry ground, that the waters of the Jordan returned to their place and went, as yesterday and the day before, over all its banks.

    soles — kap: a sole set down stopped the river (3:13); a sole lifted lets it go.

    19 And the people came up from the Jordan on the tenth of the first month, and they camped at Gilgal, at the eastern edge of Jericho.15

    20 And these twelve stones which they took from the Jordan, Joshua set up at Gilgal.

    21 And he said to the sons of Israel, saying, “When y'all's children ask their fathers tomorrow, saying, ‘What are these stones?’

    22 then y'all shall let y'all's children know, saying, ‘On dry land Israel crossed this Jordan.’

    dry land — yavesh, a different word from the dry ground of 3:17, and the one creation and the Reed Sea use.
    Gen 32:10 — Jacob, coming home: “with only my staff I crossed this Jordan.” The same words return with Israel, now a nation, as the one who crossed.

    23 For Yahweh y'all's God dried up the waters of the Jordan from before y'all until y'all had crossed, just as Yahweh y'all's God did to the Reed Sea, which he dried up from before us until we had crossed,16

    before us... we had crossed — the speaker counts his hearers, born in the wilderness, among the generation that crossed the sea.
    Matt 3:13–17 — Jesus is baptized in the Jordan. From the early centuries Christian readers took this crossing as a figure of baptism, helped by the name: in Greek, Joshua and Jesus are one name, Iēsous (“Yahweh saves”), which Heb 4:8 uses of Joshua.

    24 so that all the peoples of the land may know the hand of Yahweh, that it is strong, so that y'all may fear Yahweh y'all's God all the days.”

    strong — ḥazaq, the root of “be strong” spoken to Joshua in 1:6–9, here as an adjective: the strength belongs to Yahweh's hand.

    Notes

    1. rose early (3:1). Joshua rises early again at Jericho (6:12), after Achan (7:16) and before Ai (8:10). It becomes a small signature of his leadership, a habit that marks each decisive morning.
    2. at the end of three days (3:2). The timing is hard to fit together. Joshua announced a crossing “within three days” at 1:11, and the spies hid for three days at 2:22, which seems to fall after the camp has moved. Some readers see two accounts of the preparation laid side by side; others hear a deliberate, almost liturgical repetition. One medieval reckoning counts from Moses' death through thirty days of mourning and lands the crossing on the tenth of the first month, the date 4:19 gives independently.
    3. two thousand cubits (3:4). Roughly 900 metres, over half a mile. The gap keeps the chest visible to the whole column and keeps the people back from a holy object. One later tradition connected the figure with the distance a person may walk on the Sabbath; the verse itself gives the reason: “so that y'all may know the way.”
    4. Set yourselves apart (3:5). Some take the command practically (get ready, gather your things) since no rite is described; others hear a real consecration before an act of God. The same root marks the commander's “holy ground” at 5:15 and the command given before Achan is found out at 7:13.
    5. the chest of the covenant of the Lord of all the land (3:11). The Hebrew construction is unusual, and it can be read two ways: “the chest of the covenant of the Lord of all the land,” or with “Lord of all the land” standing beside the chest, as if naming the presence it carries. Elsewhere the chest is addressed as Yahweh himself when it sets out and comes to rest (Num 10:35–36). The title sets a universal claim inside a local, tribal procession, and 4:24 widens it to all the peoples of the land.
    6. full over all its banks all the days of harvest (3:15). The crossing falls in early spring, at barley harvest, when snowmelt from the northern mountains swells the river. The note is not decoration: this is not a low-water ford but the Jordan at its most dangerous.
    7. at Adam, the city beside Zarethan (3:16). The written text reads “at Adam”; the reading tradition says “from Adam,” so that the waters stood “very far from Adam.” Adam is usually located at Tell ed-Damiyeh, about 30 km upstream of Jericho, near where the Jabbok joins the Jordan. The banks there are high and soft, and landslides have blocked the river at that spot in recorded history, for about sixteen hours in 1267 and for about a day in 1927. Readers divide over whether such a mechanism lies behind the story; the text itself times the stoppage to the moment the priests' feet touch the water.
    8. stood fixed (3:17). The root kun, “be fixed, firm,” runs through the scene three times: the priests stand fixed (3:17), the stones are taken from where their feet stood fixed (4:3), and Joshua calls the men he had fixed on, that is, appointed (4:4). What is fixed in the riverbed becomes what is fixed for the people.
    9. when the whole nation had finished crossing (4:1). The Masoretic text places a paragraph break in the middle of this verse, after “crossing the Jordan,” closing the crossing proper before Yahweh speaks again. Chapter 4 then retells much of chapter 3 from another angle: the twelve men chosen at 3:12 without a task receive it only now, and the priests who seem to have crossed are still in the river at 4:10. Some readers see layered sources here, others a narrative that repeats itself like a liturgy.
    10. twelve stones Joshua set up in the middle of the Jordan (4:9). Two memorials, or one? The Greek version says plainly “twelve other stones,” a second set standing in the riverbed where the priests stood, alongside the set carried to Gilgal (4:20). Others read the verse as a second telling of the same stones. “To this day” is the first of the book's many notices of things that remain.
    11. the chest of Yahweh crossed, and the priests, before the people (4:11). If the people have already crossed, how does the chest cross “before” them? The phrase may mean “in the sight of the people,” or it may be the narrator's summary running ahead of the detailed account in 4:15–18. One ancient reading imagined the chest lifting its own bearers across; the plainer readings need no second miracle.
    12. the sons of Reuben and the sons of Gad and the half-tribe of Manasseh (4:12). The eastern tribes keep the promise they made to Joshua at 1:16–18, and the one Moses first required of them (Num 32:20–22): to cross armed ahead of their kin before returning to their own land.
    13. Yahweh made Joshua great (4:14). The promise of 3:7 is fulfilled here, not at the moment the waters stop but after the armed crossing and the obedience of 4:10–13. The placement credits Joshua's stature to carrying out what Yahweh and Moses commanded, rather than to the wonder itself.
    14. the chest of the testimony (4:16). The only place in Joshua where the chest takes this name, which belongs to the tabernacle chapters of Exodus (Exod 25:22; 40:3). Elsewhere in the book it is “the chest of the covenant.” Some see a different hand here; either way, the name ties the object in the riverbed to the tent of Yahweh's presence.
    15. the tenth of the first month (4:19). The day the Passover lamb is chosen and set apart (Exod 12:3). Four days later Israel keeps Passover at Gilgal on the fourteenth (5:10), in the land for the first time.
    16. as Yahweh y'all's God did to the Reed Sea (4:23). The narrator names the model outright: the Jordan is dried up as the sea was (Exod 14:21–22), “until we had crossed.” The first person plural gathers speaker and hearers into the exodus generation, although most of that generation died in the wilderness. The memory is claimed, not inherited at a distance.
