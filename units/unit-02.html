The Book of Joshua · Study Translation

Rahab and the Spies

Unit 2 · Joshua 2:1–24

    Two Men, Quietly · 2:1

    1 And Joshua son of Nun sent, from Shittim, two men — spies — quietly, saying, "Go, see the land, and Jericho." And they went, and they came into the house of a woman, a prostitute, and her name was Rahab; and they lay down there.

    quietly — ḥeresh, "in silence"; one old reading hears ḥeres, "pottery," and imagines the men disguised as pot-sellers. a woman, a prostitute, and her name was Rahab — three tags stacked on one person while the spies stay nameless; her name is the ordinary word for "wide, spacious," elsewhere used of the land itself. came into … lay down — boʾ and shakav are both ordinary verbs that can also describe sex; the narrator had a plainer "lodge" available and did not use it. What happened is left unsaid.
    Num 25:1 — at Shittim "the people began to whore with the daughters of Moab." The spies set out from the same camp.

    The King's Messengers · 2:2–7

    2 And it was told to the king of Jericho: "Look — men have come here tonight from the sons of Israel, to dig out the land."

    dig out — ḥafar, literally "dig": the informant's verb for spying is not Joshua's "see."

    3 And the king of Jericho sent to Rahab, saying, "Bring out the men who came to you, who came into your house — for it is to dig out all the land that they have come."

    4 And the woman took the two men and hid him. And she said, "Yes — the men came to me, but I did not know where they were from.

    hid him — the Hebrew object is singular though two men are hidden. Kept as it stands: one reading has her hide each man separately, another has only one needing to be hidden at all.

    5 And when the gate was about to shut, in the dark, the men went out. I do not know where the men went. Chase after them, fast — y'all will overtake them."

    6 But she had brought them up to the roof and hidden them away among the stalks of flax she had laid out on the roof.

    7 And the men chased after them on the Jordan road, as far as the fords; and the gate — they shut it just after the chasers had gone out after them.

    the fords — maʿbarot, "crossing-places," from the same root as the Jordan-crossing verb that runs through the book.

    What We Heard · 2:8–14

    8 And they — before they lay down — she went up to them, on the roof.

    9 And she said to the men, "I know that Yahweh has given y'all the land, and that dread of y'all has fallen on us, and that all who live in the land have gone soft before y'all.

    Exod 15:15–16 — "all the inhabitants of Canaan melted away; terror and dread fell upon them." She speaks Israel's own song back to Israel.

    10 For we heard how Yahweh dried up the waters of the Reed Sea before y'all when y'all came out of Egypt, and what y'all did to the two kings of the Amorites on the far side of the Jordan — to Sihon and to Og — whom y'all devoted to destruction.

    devoted to destruction — ḥaram, the book's first use of the word, and it belongs to the woman about to be exempted from it. Rendering provisional: the English for this word is not yet fixed. Reed Sea — yam suf, literally "sea of reeds."

    11 And we heard, and our heart melted, and no spirit rose any longer in anyone before y'all — because Yahweh, y'all's God, he is God in the skies above and on the earth beneath.

    in the skies above — shamayim, rendered "skies" throughout, including here where the phrase is a creed rather than a weather report.
    Deut 4:39 — "Yahweh, he is God in the skies above and on the earth beneath; there is no other." Deuteronomy's confession, from a Canaanite mouth.

    12 And now, swear to me, please, by Yahweh — since I have done ḥesed with y'all — that y'all too will do ḥesed with my father's house, and give me a sign of truth,

    ḥesed — left in Hebrew: loyalty that binds both sides, the kind a covenant partner owes and keeps. One reading splits the request in two: saving Rahab repays a debt, but saving her family, who did nothing for the spies, is ḥesed freely given — which is why she asks it for "my father's house."

    13 and keep alive my father and my mother, my brothers and my sisters, and all that is theirs, and snatch our beings from death."

    my sisters — the written text has "my sister," the read text "my sisters." our beings — nefesh in the plural, rendered "beings"; in the singular (2:14) it is "life."

    14 And the men said to her, "Our life in place of y'all's, to die — if y'all do not tell this business of ours. And it will be, when Yahweh gives us the land, we will do ḥesed and truth with you."

    if y'all do not tell — plural, though they are speaking to her: the silence is laid on the whole household. At 2:20 the same condition is singular.

    The Cord in the Window · 2:15–21

    15 And she brought them down by a rope through the window — for her house was in the side of the wall, and in the wall she was living.1

    in the side of the wall — two words for "wall" together; a house built into the fortification itself.

    16 And she said to them, "To the hill country — go, so the chasers don't run into y'all; and hide there three days, until the chasers have returned, and afterward y'all can go y'all's way."

    17 And the men said to her, "Clear are we of this oath of yours that you made us swear —

    clear — naqi, "innocent, released": the spies say it three times (2:17, 19, 20), opening and closing their terms by disowning the oath as hers.

    18 look: we are coming into the land. This cord of scarlet thread you will tie in the window you brought us down through; and your father and your mother and your brothers and all your father's house you will gather to yourself, into the house.

    cord — tiqvah, which also means "hope"; and "scarlet," shani, sounds like "two." The sign puns on what it gives her and on who gave it.
    Exod 12:21–23 — a red mark at the opening, the household gathered inside, no one going out until morning, and the destroyer passes over.

    19 And it will be: anyone who goes out of the doors of your house into the street — his blood is on his head, and we are clear; and anyone who is with you in the house — his blood is on our head, if a hand is on him.

    20 And if you tell this business of ours, we will be clear of your oath that you made us swear."

    21 And she said, "As y'all say — so it is." And she sent them off, and they went; and she tied the scarlet cord in the window.

    The Report · 2:22–24

    22 And they went, and came to the hill country, and stayed there three days, until the chasers returned; and the chasers searched the whole road, and did not find.2

    23 And the two men turned back, and came down from the hill country, and crossed over, and came to Joshua son of Nun, and recounted to him everything that had found them.

    everything that had found them — matsaʾ, the verb of 2:22: the chasers did not find the men, but what happened did.

    24 And they said to Joshua, "Yahweh has given into our hand all the land; and also, all who live in the land have gone soft before us."

    Notes

    1. Excavations at the site identified with Jericho have found fortifications with houses built on and into the defensive slope, which makes a house "in the wall" architecturally plausible. Whether any destruction layer there fits the conquest remains disputed: the main destruction has been dated both to the sixteenth century BC and to about 1400 BC, and current excavation has not settled which stratum, if any, corresponds to the story.
    2. The spies' three days in the hills do not fit easily into the three-day countdown of 1:11 and 3:2. One reading counts the days so the spies were sent during the mourning for Moses; another treats "three days" as a link between the stories rather than a timetable.
