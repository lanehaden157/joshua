The Book of Joshua · Study Translation

Rights of Passage

Unit 1 · Joshua 1:1–18

    Yahweh commissions Joshua · 1:1–9

    1 And it was after the death of Moses, slave of Yahweh, that Yahweh said to Joshua son of Nun, Moses' attendant:12

    attendant — mesharet, Joshua's title from the wilderness: Moses is the slave of Yahweh, Joshua the slave's aide.
    Judg 1:1 opens with the same clause, wayehi ʾaḥarei mot ("and it was after the death of"), for Joshua; the book stands between two deaths.

    2 "Moses my slave is dead. And now rise, cross over this Jordan, you and all this people, to the land that I am giving to them, to the sons of Israel.

    I am giving — a participle, the gift under way; the next verse says "I have given."
    Deut 34:5–9 ends with Moses, slave of Yahweh, dead in Moab and Israel listening to Joshua; this verse picks up the notice in Yahweh's own voice and moves on.
    Deut 3:27 and 31:2: "you shall not cross this Jordan" — Moses was refused exactly the verb and object Joshua now receives.

    3 Every place that the sole of y'all's foot treads on, to y'all I have given it, as I spoke to Moses.3

    The gift is measured by the footprint: not "all this land," but wherever a foot comes down.
    Deut 11:24 — Moses' sermon at the edge of the land promised the same ground by the same footprint and the same borders, as a future gift; here it is spoken as already given.
    Gen 13:17 — Abraham was told to walk the length and breadth of the land because it would be given to him; walking the ground is how the gift is claimed.

    4 From the wilderness and this Lebanon to the great river, the river Euphrates — all the land of the Hittites — and to the Great Sea where the sun goes in: that will be y'all's border.45

    Hittites — here the great northern region of Syria, not one of Canaan's peoples. This Lebanon — as if in view from the Jordan valley.

    5 No one will take a stand before you all the days of your life. As I was with Moses, I will be with you; I will not drop you, and I will not abandon you.

    No one will take a stand — kept at 21:44 and 23:9: "no one stood before them."
    Deut 31:6, 8 — "he will not drop you or abandon you" was Moses' word about Yahweh, to the people and then to Joshua; "I will be with you" was said to Moses at the bush (Exod 3:12), to Isaac, to Jacob and later to Gideon. Here Yahweh says both in the first person.
    Heb 13:5 quotes this promise to a later community, in the wording of Deuteronomy's Greek rather than this chapter's.

    6 Be strong and firm, for you will cause this people to inherit the land that I swore to their fathers to give them.

    cause … to inherit — one Hebrew word, tanḥil, the first of the inheritance family in the book.

    Moses commissions Joshua Num 27:15–23; Deut 3:28; 31:1–8, 14–15, 23

    Facing his death, Moses asks Yahweh to appoint a shepherd so the congregation will not be like sheep without one, and Joshua is commissioned by the laying on of Moses' hands before the priest Eleazar and all the people. In Deuteronomy Moses charges Joshua before all Israel, "be strong and firm, for you will go in with this people … and you will cause them to inherit it," and then Yahweh charges him again at the tent of meeting, in the pillar of cloud.

    • "be strong and firm" (ḥazaq weʾemats)
    • "you will cause them to inherit" (tanḥil)
    • "I will be with you"

    There, Moses speaks, a priest stands by, hands are laid on and all Israel watches. Here Yahweh speaks directly and alone, with no priest, no hands and no audience, and Moses is already dead.

    It is Yahweh's first direct word to Joshua in the book, and it confirms the successor in the terms Moses had used: the commission is no longer passed on, it is given.

    Joshua passes the same charge to his commanders at 10:25: "do not fear, do not be shattered, be strong and firm."

    1 Kgs 2:2–3; 1 Chr 22:13; 28:20 — David charges Solomon with the same "be strong and firm," the same keeping of Moses' torah and the same promise that God will not drop him or abandon him.

    7 Only be strong and very firm, to keep and do according to all the torah that Moses my slave commanded you. Do not turn from it right or left, so that you may have insight wherever you go.6

    torah — "instruction," left untranslated. have insight — the word means both seeing clearly and succeeding.
    Deut 17:18–20 — the king is to write his own copy of the torah, read it all the days of his life, keep and do it, and not turn right or left; Joshua, who is no king, receives the king's instruction.

    8 This scroll of the torah shall not depart from your mouth; you shall murmur it day and night, so that you keep and do according to all that is written in it. For then you will make your way prosper, and then you will have insight.

    murmur — reading half-aloud, the scroll kept "in the mouth." Whether "shall not depart" is a command or a blessing is read both ways.

    The young attendant who did not leave the tent Exod 33:7–11; Num 11:26–29

    Moses pitches the tent of meeting outside the camp, and when he goes in, the pillar of cloud comes down at the entrance and Yahweh speaks with him face to face. When Moses goes back to the camp, his attendant Joshua son of Nun, a young man, does not depart from the tent. Later, when two men prophesy in the camp, it is Joshua who asks Moses to stop them.

    • Joshua as Moses' attendant (mesharet)
    • the verb "depart" (mush): Joshua "did not depart" from the tent; the scroll "shall not depart" from his mouth
    • "day and night": the pillar of cloud and fire "did not depart" from the people by day or by night (Exod 13:21–22)

    Then Joshua stayed near Yahweh's voice by staying in a place; now he must keep it by keeping a written scroll in his mouth.

    Joshua's inheritance of Moses moves from presence by nearness to presence by text: the scroll takes the place of the tent and the pillar.

    At the book's end Joshua himself writes in "the scroll of the torah of God" (24:26).

    Ps 1:2–3 — the happy person delights in Yahweh's torah, murmurs it day and night, and prospers in all he does; the Prophets and the Writings each open with this same instruction.

    9 Have I not commanded you? Be strong and firm. Do not be terrified, and do not be shattered, for Yahweh your God is with you wherever you go."

    Joshua hands the whole set on to his commanders at 10:25: "do not fear, do not be shattered, be strong and firm."
    Matt 28:18–20 — the risen Jesus, bearing Joshua's own name, may be heard closing a mountain commission much as Yahweh opens this one: "teaching them to keep all that I commanded you," and "I am with you all the days."

    Joshua's order to the scribes · 1:10–11

    10 And Joshua commanded the scribes of the people, saying,

    scribes — shoterim, the officials who kept the muster rolls and relayed orders.

    11 "Pass through the middle of the camp and command the people, saying, 'Prepare provisions for y'all, for in three days more y'all are crossing this Jordan, to go in to take possession of the land that Yahweh y'all's God is giving y'all to possess it.'"78

    The relay — Yahweh, Joshua, scribes, people — is repeated almost word for word when the crossing begins (3:2–4).

    Reuben, Gad and half of Manasseh · 1:12–18

    12 And to the Reubenite, and to the Gadite, and to the half-tribe of Manasseh, Joshua said,

    The bargain for the east bank Num 32:1–42; Deut 3:12–20

    Reuben and Gad, rich in cattle, ask Moses for the grazing land east of the Jordan and ask not to be taken across. Moses is furious: they will discourage the others, as the spies did at Kadesh. Then he agrees on one condition: their fighting men will cross armed ahead of their brothers until Yahweh gives them rest, and only then go home. The tribes pledge, "your slaves will do as my lord commands," and half of Manasseh is added to the grant.

    • "wives, little ones and livestock" stay in the east
    • the fighting men cross armed before their brothers
    • "until Yahweh gives y'all's brothers rest as he has y'all"
    • the tribes' pledge to do all that is commanded

    In Numbers the tribes volunteer and Moses bargains; here Joshua simply calls the deal in as a word already given, and the tribes answer him in their own earlier words.

    The chapter's last scene turns on the one group that does not receive land at Joshua's hand: their loyalty is promised as an obligation, not earned by an inheritance.

    Joshua releases them at 22:1–6, and the altar they build at the Jordan brings Israel to the edge of civil war (22:10–34).

    13 "Remember the word that Moses, slave of Yahweh, commanded y'all, saying, 'Yahweh y'all's God is giving y'all rest, and he has given y'all this land.'

    Remember — Deuteronomy's covenant verb; the tribes are held to a word already spoken.
    Heb 4:8 names Joshua outright (Iēsous, "Jesus"): if Joshua had given them rest, God would not have spoken later of another day.

    14 Y'all's wives, y'all's little ones and y'all's livestock shall stay in the land that Moses gave y'all across the Jordan; but y'all shall cross over battle-ready before y'all's brothers, all the mighty men of valor, and help them,9

    across the Jordan — said as if from the west bank, though Joshua stands on the east. "Moses gave" here; "Yahweh is giving" beside it.

    15 until Yahweh gives rest to y'all's brothers as he has to y'all, and they too take possession of the land that Yahweh y'all's God is giving them. Then y'all shall return to the land of y'all's possession and possess it, which Moses, slave of Yahweh, gave y'all across the Jordan, toward the sunrise."1011

    The rest promised here is declared given at 21:44, 22:4 and 23:1.

    16 And they answered Joshua, saying, "All that you have commanded us we will do, and wherever you send us we will go.

    Singular "you" now: the tribes address Joshua alone, in Yahweh's own terms.
    Exod 19:8; 24:7 — at Sinai the people answered "we will do" and "we will listen"; the tribes answer Joshua with the same two verbs, in the same order (here and in the next verse).

    17 Just as we listened to Moses in everything, so we will listen to you. Only may Yahweh your God be with you, as he was with Moses.12

    18 Any man who rebels against your mouth and does not listen to your words, in all that you command him, shall be put to death. Only be strong and firm."13

    The chapter ends with Yahweh's own charge, "be strong and firm," handed back to Joshua by the people least bound to him.

    Notes

    1. slave of Yahweh (v1). Judges opens with the same clause after Joshua's death, so this book is framed by two death notices. Joshua is Moses' "attendant" here and is given Moses' title, "slave of Yahweh," only when he dies (24:29). The Greek translation lacks "slave of Yahweh" here and at 1:15, 12:6 and 22:4. Several explanations are offered: a scribe's eye skipping, a later expansion in Deuteronomy's style, the growing honour paid to Moses, and the Greek translator's habit of shortening. The question is left open.
    2. after the death of Moses (v1). The chapter passes over the thirty days of mourning at the end of Deuteronomy in a single clause. One Jewish tradition holds that Yahweh's word could not come to Joshua while he grieved, and that the commission came on the day the mourning ended.
    3. every place … I have given (v3). The promise is not "all this land" but "every place the sole of y'all's foot treads." The book later summarizes the conquest as complete ("all the land," "not one word failed," 11:23; 21:43–45), while 13:1 says "very much land remains" and Judges 1 lists the towns never taken. Read back from those passages, the footprint clause holds the tension in advance: the land is given where it is walked.
    4. y'all's border (v4). These are Deuteronomy's maximal borders, reaching the Euphrates and Lebanon, not the smaller map of Canaan in Numbers 34. Israel's reach touches the Euphrates only under David and Solomon and loses it again; Josh 13:5 lists "all the Lebanon" among the land that remains.
    5. from the wilderness and this Lebanon (v4). The chapter of Deuteronomy that supplies these borders (Deut 11) goes on to order the blessing-and-curse ceremony on Mount Gerizim and Mount Ebal, which Joshua performs at 8:30–35 "as Moses commanded." The book's opening promise and its first covenant ceremony draw on the same page.
    6. only (v7). raq ("only") limits the charge: the strength asked for is strength to keep the torah, not strength in battle. The word returns at 22:5 ("only keep diligently") and twice in the tribes' reply (1:17, 1:18).
    7. provisions (v11). One medieval reading takes tsedah here as gear and weapons rather than food, since the manna did not stop until after the crossing (5:12). The word returns in the Gibeonite ruse, where Israel takes the strangers' worn-out provisions "and did not ask Yahweh's mouth" (9:5, 9:11, 9:14).
    8. in three days more (v11). The spies hide in the hills three days (2:16, 2:22), and the crossing begins "at the end of three days" (3:2), so the chapters overlap or leave a gap. One medieval calendar reconciles them: Moses dies on the seventh of Adar, the mourning ends on the seventh of Nisan, the order is given that day, the spies are sent during the mourning, and the crossing comes on the tenth (4:19). That is a solution offered by readers, not the text's own account.
    9. battle-ready (v14). ḥamushim is rare. It is read as "armed," "equipped," or "in battle formation," and one reading connects it with the number five, an army arranged in five divisions. The exodus army is given the same word (Exod 13:18).
    10. the tribes' standing (v12–15). Joshua's speech alternates between "the land Moses gave y'all" and "the land Yahweh is giving." One reading hears in the alternation the eastern tribes' uncertain place: united with Israel, yet holding land that sits outside the gift across the Jordan. Read that way, the surface unity of this chapter looks ahead to the crisis over the altar at the Jordan (22:10–34).
    11. across the Jordan, toward the sunrise (v15). Scholars read the chapter's history in three ways: as a single bridge written to join Deuteronomy to Joshua; as an older core (the commands to cross and prepare) expanded in Deuteronomy's style; or as the opening of a book that was once independent and was set into its present place late. The west-bank vantage of "across the Jordan" is one piece of evidence in that debate.
    12. only may Yahweh be with you (v17). The tribes' two "only"s (here and v18) are read two ways. One reading hears a hedge: their loyalty holds on condition that Yahweh is with Joshua as he was with Moses. Another reads raq as a plain "however," a closing wish and charge with no reservation in it. The chapter does not settle which.
    13. rebels against your mouth (v18). The verb for "rebel" is the one used elsewhere for rebellion against Yahweh's own command, so the tribes treat Joshua's word as carrying Yahweh's authority. The death penalty recalls Deuteronomy's laws on refusing to listen to the priest, the judge (Deut 17:12) and the prophet like Moses (18:19). The phrase itself also occurs at Numbers 20:24, where Yahweh says Moses and Aaron "rebelled against my mouth" at Meribah, and that rebellion is why Moses may not cross. The tribes pledge to Joshua at the Jordan's edge the obedience for which Moses was barred from it.
